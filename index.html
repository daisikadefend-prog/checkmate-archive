01 · THE GOAL

How to play chess

Chess is played by two sides on an 8×8 board. White moves first. The aim is to checkmate the opposing king, which means the king is under attack and has no legal escape. You do not capture the king.

Set the board with a light square in each player's right-hand corner. Put rooks in the corners, knights beside them, bishops next, then place the queen on her own colour and the king on the remaining square.

  • White moves first, then players alternate turns.
  • You may not make a move that leaves your own king in check.
  • A game can end by checkmate, draw, resignation or timeout.

02 · PIECE MOVEMENT

How each piece moves

♔ KingOne square in any direction.
♕ QueenAny distance in a straight line or diagonal.
♖ RookAny distance horizontally or vertically.
♗ BishopAny distance diagonally. It stays on one square colour.
♘ KnightAn L-shape: two squares, then one sideways. It can jump.
♙ PawnMoves forward, captures diagonally, and may promote on the last rank.

03 · CHECKMATE

How to find checkmate

Look for forcing moves first: checks, captures and direct threats. Then count the opponent's safe king squares. A mating move works only when the king cannot move away, capture the attacker or block the check.

Ask: Where can the king move? Can the checking piece be captured? Can the line of attack be blocked?

04 · TACTICS

Spot the common patterns

ForkOne piece attacks two or more targets at once.
PinA piece cannot safely move because a more valuable piece is behind it.
SkewerA valuable piece is attacked and moves, exposing another target.
Discovered attackMoving one piece opens an attack from a piece behind it.
Double checkTwo pieces give check at once. The king must move.

05 · BEGINNER PLAN

Play a stronger opening

  1. Control the centre with pawns and pieces.
  2. Develop knights and bishops before moving the same piece repeatedly.
  3. Castle early when it is safe to do so.
  4. Connect your rooks and avoid leaving pieces undefended.
  5. Before every move, check what your opponent is attacking.

A useful thinking routine: checks, captures, threats. Consider your opponent's forcing moves before choosing yours.
